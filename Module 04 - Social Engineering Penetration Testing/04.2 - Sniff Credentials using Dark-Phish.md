### Scenario

Dark-Phish is a specialized phishing tool designed for educational purposes and security testing. It is capable of simulating phishing attacks and facilitating the evaluation of system vulnerabilities and user awareness levels.

Here, we will sniff user credentials using Dark-Phish.

### Objective

The objective of this lab is to help students learn how to sniff user credentials using Dark-Phish by hosting a local phishing server and harvesting credentials from a remote target machine.

---

## Task 1: Sniffing Credentials using Dark-Phish

In this task, we will configure and launch Dark-Phish on the Parrot Security machine to capture victim credentials using a simulated Google sign-in page.

### Lab Tasks
1. On **Parrot Security**, open a Terminal window, and execute <font color="#ffd700"><b>sudo su</b></font> to run the programs as a root user (When prompted, enter the password <font color="#ff4d4d"><b>toor</b></font>).
2. Run <font color="#ffd700"><b>cd Dark-Phish/</b></font> to navigate to the Dark-Phish directory.
3. In this task, we will be hosting the PHP server on the local host. To do so, we need to change the localhost IP address in the <font color="#c084fc"><b>dark-phish.py</b></font> file.
4. In the terminal window, type <font color="#ffd700"><b>pluma dark-phish.py</b></font> to edit the Python script in the Pluma text editor.
5. In the text editor, replace `127.0.0.1` with <font color="#38bdf8"><b>172.19.19.18</b></font> in line 30 under the host field, save the file, and close the text editor window.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/jqvyc2l4.jpg)

6. Launch Dark-Phish by executing the <font color="#ffd700"><b>python3 dark-phish.py</b></font> command.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/vikc40ue.jpg)

7. The Dark-Phish menu appears as shown in the screenshot. You can observe numerous platform options presented by Dark-Phish. Now, type <font color="#ffd700"><b>06</b></font> and press Enter to choose **Google**.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/go04jcwt.jpg)

8. A list of hosting options appears; type <font color="#ffd700"><b>01</b></font> and hit Enter to choose **Localhost**.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/h43zsukr.jpg)

9. Modify the URL (Y/N) option appears; type <font color="#ffd700"><b>N</b></font> and press Enter.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/lc2go3c5.jpg)

10. Send link appears. Having successfully generated a phishing link, you must now send this link to a victim and try to trick them into clicking it. So, copy the link.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/zrrq0ye2.jpg)

11. Launch a web browser (here, Mozilla Firefox) and open your email account (in this example, we are using Outlook). Log in and compose an email.
    - *(You can log in to any email service of your preference.)*
12. After logging into your email account, click the **New Mail** button in the left pane and compose a fake but enticing email to lure a user into opening the email and clicking on a malicious link.
13. Select "here" to place the fake URL, then switch to Messages from the toolbar and click the **Insert link** icon.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/dp13.jpg)

14. Paste the copied Send link in the Insert link window under the Web address (URL) field and click **OK**.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/9uucwbor.jpg)

15. To verify the URL, hover over the hyperlink, and the full URL will appear. Once verified, send the email to the intended user.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/6ssbrrk6.jpg)

16. Click **Sales Department** to switch to the Sales Department machine. Login with <font color="#ff4d4d"><b>Admin</b></font> / <font color="#ff4d4d"><b>test@123</b></font>.
17. Open the Google Chrome browser and sign in to the email account to which you sent the phishing mail as an attacker. Open the email you sent previously and click to open the malicious link.
    - *(If the email does not appear in your Inbox, check the Spam folder. If a pop-up appears, click Open anyway.)*

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/o8e226ol.jpg)

18. When the victim (you in this case) clicks the URL, a new tab opens, and displays a replica of the Google sign-in page.
19. The victim will be prompted to enter the username and password into the form fields, which appear as they do on a legitimate website. When the victim enters the Username and Password and signs into it, they will be redirected to the legitimate Google page. Note the different URLs in the browser address bar for the cloned and legitimate sites.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/xqhv51sa.jpg)

20. Now, click **Parrot Security** to switch back to the Parrot Security machine and switch to the terminal window. Soon after the victim clicks Sign in, Dark-Phish records the credentials. The attacker can then use these to gain unauthorized access to the victim's account.
21. The captured credentials are displayed in the terminal.

![Screenshot](../Lab%20Manuals/Module%2004_%20Social%20Engineering%20Penetration%20Testing_files/p9p97ix0.jpg)
